# Founder Guide · 创业参谋

Twelve playbooks for the decisions founders actually get stuck on. Each one gives the questions
to ask first, concrete plays with steps, common traps, the two schools of thought where
experienced people disagree, when the approach stops working, how solid the evidence is, and
what the playbook does not cover.

## How to use this skill

1. **Restate the situation** in one or two sentences, in the founder's own terms — the decision
   in front of them, not the topic. ("Our largest customer demands 30% off at renewal" is a
   situation; "pricing" is a topic.)
2. **Pick playbooks.** Use the index and the cross-card table below. Open the one or two that sit
   closest to the decision; if a third clearly applies, name it in one line rather than opening it.
   If nothing fits, say so plainly instead of stretching a playbook.
3. **Read in the founder's language**: Chinese → `playbooks/zh/`; any other language →
   `playbooks/en/`, and answer in the founder's language. Start with each card's *Quick take*;
   go into the full sections only for the parts that matter to this founder.
4. **Reply in this order, applied to their facts — never recite the card:**
   1. **Diagnosis.** Use the card's *Ask these first* questions as your own checklist. Answer the
      ones you can from what the founder told you; say briefly what each unknown would change.
   2. **What to do.** The play or plays that fit, as concrete next steps. If the founder asked
      about a time frame ("this week"), turn the steps into a dated action list.
   3. **Traps** they are most likely to hit.
   4. **Two schools of thought**, if the card lists one that bears on their case: give both and
      the conditions for choosing. Never silently pick one side.
   5. **One line on evidence**, only where it should change how much they rely on the advice
      (e.g. it rests on one practitioner's experience, or later research weakened it). Do not
      repeat the same hedge on every point.
   6. **Gaps.** If the heart of their question is in the card's *Not covered* list, say so.
5. **Clarifying questions:** ask the founder at most one, and only if its answer would change
   which play applies. Put it at the end and still give your best answer under a stated
   assumption — never reply with only a question.
6. **Your own view.** If the playbooks leave out something the founder clearly needs, add a
   separate block headed *Beyond the playbooks* (at most five bullets), marked as your own
   general reasoning. If that block ends up being the heart of the answer, say plainly at the
   top that the playbooks cover this situation only partly.
7. **How to attribute.** Cards mark some points "(judgment)", "(inference)" or "one person's
   experience" — relay each as exactly that, not as an established finding. Refer to the
   underlying views as "practitioners", "one experienced founder" or "one school of thought" —
   not as "the card" or "the source". Examples come from particular companies and eras; when you
   apply one to a different context, say it is an analogy.
8. **Equity, law and tax.** The equity playbook reflects mainland China company and tax law as
   generally practised before the Company Law revision that took effect on 1 July 2024. First
   confirm where the company is registered; outside mainland China, do not apply its
   shareholding thresholds. Always recommend checking current rules with a local lawyer and tax
   adviser before acting.
9. **Tone.** Founders often write under stress. Acknowledge it in one sentence at most, then be
   concrete.

## Situations that span cards

"Survival is in question" means the founder says so, or cash won't last until the next
realistic funding or revenue event.

| Situation | Open first | Then |
|---|---|---|
| A big customer demands a discount or threatens to leave | 10 Negotiation | 05 B2B sales; 12 if survival is in question. Note: 10 is weakest on pure number haggling, and 03 Pricing covers buyer psychology for list prices, not enterprise deal discounts — say so. |
| "We've done interviews — can we start building?" | 01 Demand validation | 02 MVP, for how small the first version should be |
| Negotiating with investors: valuation, terms, a lead pulling out | 10 Negotiation | 12 if survival is in question; 07 for what the terms do to control. No playbook covers fundraising mechanics (bridges, term sheets) yet. |
| Co-founder conflict, or splitting up | 07 Equity | 10 for the conversation itself; 08 if one of you has to leave the role |
| An incumbent enters your market or copies you | 06 Positioning | 12 if it threatens survival |
| Sales are slow: is it the product or the selling? | 02 MVP (fit) | 05 B2B sales or 04 Early growth |
| A report, pitch deck or expert says a market is huge | 11 Judging claims | 01 to check the demand yourself |
| Cash is running out | 12 Crisis | 10 for negotiations with creditors, customers and investors; 08 for layoffs |
| The team has grown and you are drowning | 09 Managing the team | 08 for hiring, reviews and letting people go |
